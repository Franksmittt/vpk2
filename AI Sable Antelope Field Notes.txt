Reference-Grade Field Notes for AI Image Generation: Hippotragus niger
1) Taxonomy and scope
The sable antelope (Hippotragus niger, formally described by Harris in 1838 from a type specimen collected in the Magaliesberg region of South Africa) is a large, heavily built bovid belonging to the subfamily Hippotraginae.1 The genus name Hippotragus translates to "horse-goat", a highly accurate morphological descriptor for a ruminant that possesses an equine build, a stiff dorsal mane, and a robust, deep-chested physiology.2 This taxonomic grouping also includes the closely related roan antelope (Hippotragus equinus) and the extinct bluebuck (Hippotragus leucophaeus), separating them phylogenetically from the Alcelaphini tribe (wildebeests and hartebeests) and the Tragelaphini tribe (kudus and nyalas).2
For the precise requirements of AI latent space navigation, the isolated term "sable" introduces catastrophic semantic ambiguity. In zoological, artistic, and generative AI contexts, the unmoored keyword "sable" overwhelmingly defaults to the sable marten (Martes zibellina), a small carnivorous mustelid of the taiga, or it triggers the rendering of generic black coat colours in domestic dogs and horses.2 To prevent this latent space bleed, prompt engineers must rigorously anchor generation strings using the full binomial nomenclature (Hippotragus niger) coupled with explicit regional and phenotypic qualifiers such as "Southern African savanna", "bushveld", or "Highveld".
The geographic scope of this monograph focuses specifically on the southern sable antelope (Hippotragus niger niger). This is the nominate subspecies and the dominant phenotype naturally occurring and actively managed within South African hunting reserves, commercial game ranches, and mixed woodland estates.1 Historically, this phenotype ranged from the northern Limpopo province, through Mpumalanga, and into the North West province of South Africa.1 The southern phenotype is characterized by pronounced sexual dimorphism, with mature bulls exhibiting a glossy jet-black coat, while females and subadults range from rich chestnut to dark brown.5 Prompting for this specific subspecies ensures the AI generates the appropriate contrasting facial masks and horn geometries required for commercial hunting reserve visual assets.
Estate Colour Morphs and Industry Relevance
The South African game ranching industry has actively manipulated sable genetics over the past three decades to produce phenotypes with exaggerated horn lengths and unique pelage traits.8 Pure "Matsetsi" sable, indigenous to South Africa and Zimbabwe, have frequently been crossbred with the "West Zambian" sable (Hippotragus niger kirkii). This phenotype is prized for exceptionally long horns and a distinctively dark facial mask.10 This intensive breeding was historically an attempt to replicate the phenotype of the critically endangered Giant Sable (Hippotragus niger variani) of Angola, though evolutionary genomics later proved these are distinct lineages.11 Furthermore, the industry has selectively bred recessive genetic traits to produce "Golden Sable" colour morphs.9 While these variants carry massive economic weight in the trophy auction market, they are structurally identical to the wild phenotype.9 Generative AI models should treat these variants purely as colour palette shifts applied over the exact same skeletal, muscular, and horn attachment geometry. The structural rules established in this document remain absolute regardless of the requested colour morph.
2) Confusion pairs

Generative models construct images based on semantic proximity, meaning that requesting an African antelope with curved horns will frequently cause the model to blend traits from multiple species found in the same geographic training data. Understanding the precise anatomical differences is the only way to write effective negative prompts and guide the diffusion process toward a pure Hippotragus niger generation.

	3) Sexual dimorphism
The sable antelope represents one of the most distinctly sexually dimorphic members of the Bovidae family, a biological reality that must be rigorously enforced in image generation to prevent anatomical blending.14 The differences extend far beyond simple scale, encompassing profound variations in horn geometry, pelage colouration, and muscular hypertrophy driven by endocrine functions.
Both sexes possess horns, which immediately distinguishes the female sable from hornless females of species like the greater kudu or nyala.15 However, the horn morphology differs drastically between the sexes. A mature male's horns are massive, deeply annulated (ridged) along the upper surface, and sweep backward in a pronounced scimitar curve.5 Male horn growth is highly aggressive during the first 15 months of life, averaging 0.89 cm per day, and continues robustly until the animal reaches approximately 1400 days of age.17 Record-class male horns reach lengths between 81 and 165 cm.5 The basal circumference of a male's horns is heavily built, measuring between 15 and 17 cm, providing the structural foundation necessary for violent intraspecific combat.17 Female horns are noticeably more slender, exhibit a much shallower backward curve, and typically only reach lengths of 61 to 102 cm.5 Female horn growth begins to plateau much earlier, around 800 days of age, as nutrients are systematically diverted away from secondary sexual characteristics and toward reproductive functions such as pregnancy and lactation.17
Pelage colour provides the most immediate visual cue for ageing and sexing the animal in the field. Calves are born a light reddish-brown, an evolutionary adaptation that provides vital camouflage in the tall, dry grass of the savanna.7 As females mature, their coats darken to a rich chestnut or dark brown, maintaining a stark visual contrast with their brilliant white facial masks, bellies, and rump patches.5 Males undergo a profound, testosterone-driven colour shift. At roughly three years of age, male coats transition rapidly from chestnut to a deep, glossy jet black.7 This melanism is directly linked to hormone production. Veterinary literature notes that a jet-black male castrated at four months of age reverted entirely to a red, calf-like coat colour within six months due to the sudden absence of testosterone.17
Structurally, males are significantly heavier and taller, weighing up to 235 kg with a shoulder height approaching 140 cm, compared to the mature female's 220 kg mass.2 Both sexes feature a stiff, upright black mane running down the dorsal ridge of the neck, but the male's neck is considerably thicker.7 Males utilize this hypertrophied musculature to adopt a highly flexed, arched-neck posture, holding their heads high to exaggerate their physical size and project dominance to rival males within the herd hierarchy.22
4) Morphology shot guide (C1–C13)
C1: Full body
A full-body shot of a sable antelope must capture its robust, barrel-chested, equine-like physiology. The camera angle should ideally be at shoulder height to prevent forced perspective from artificially shrinking the hindquarters or elongating the legs. The animal must stand firmly on four legs, displaying the deep chest, the thick neck, and the straight, strong backline that defines the Hippotragus genus. In the South African bushveld context, the lighting should be angled to catch the glossy sheen of the coat, separating the dark torso from the muted greens, ochres, and browns of the background vegetation.
The stance should highlight the signature arched neck, particularly if the subject is a mature bull displaying dominance. The tail must hang naturally, reaching down toward the hocks, terminating in a distinct dark tuft.2 The bright white underbelly must form a sharp, clean anatomical line against the dark flanks, without any soft gradient bleeding between the two colours. This sharp contrast is a hallmark of the species and is critical for reference-grade imagery.
* Must show: Deep barrel chest, stiff dorsal mane, clear separation of white belly and dark flank, exactly four distinct cloven-hooved legs.
* Must avoid: Sloping wildebeest-like backs, shaggy ventral fringes (nyala traits), or long shaggy coats.
* AI failure modes: The AI frequently merges the front legs into a single thick pillar or generates five legs when the subject is standing in tall grass. The latent space may also attempt to append a horse's flowing mane instead of the stiff, bristly mane of the antelope. Ensure the backline remains level and does not droop into a hyena-like slope.
C2: Eyes
The eye of the sable antelope is a masterpiece of prey-animal adaptation and must be rendered with strict adherence to ruminant anatomy. Placed laterally on the skull to afford a panoramic visual field covering up to 340 degrees, the eye features a distinctively horizontal pupil.23 When dilated in low light, this pupil appears almost rectangular, an evolutionary trait designed to scan the horizon for predators without requiring head movement.23 The iris is a natural, deep mammalian brown. The eye is set within the dark bands of the facial mask, often with a subtle white orbital ring or contrasting white fur immediately adjacent to the dark stripes.
Lighting should ideally catch a subtle reflection in the upper quadrant of the eye to give the animal a sense of life, but it must not illuminate the eye from within (avoiding the glowing tapetum lucidum effect unless the scene is explicitly set at night). The surrounding skin and eyelashes are coarse and dark. The preorbital gland, located just in front of the eye, is present but should remain subtle. It must not be overly exaggerated or depicted as an open, weeping wound.
* Must show: Lateral eye placement, deep brown iris, horizontal pupil, dark surrounding skin integrating seamlessly into the facial mask.
* Must avoid: Forward-facing predator eyes, round human-like pupils, excessive scleral show (white of the eye).
* AI failure modes: Generative models overwhelmingly default to human-like or domestic dog-like eyes due to training data bias. The most critical failure modes include generating bright blue eyes, heterochromia, or perfectly circular pupils. The AI may also incorrectly place the eyes on the front of the face, destroying the elongated prey-animal skull structure.
C3: Horns
Sable horns are the defining trophy characteristic for the hunting reserve audience and must be rendered with absolute architectural precision. The horns insert directly into the frontal bones of the skull, just above and slightly behind the orbits. They rise vertically before sweeping backward in a continuous, graceful arc.7 The curvature exists strictly in a single backward plane. There is absolutely no lateral twisting, flaring, or corkscrew spiralling.
The lower two-thirds to three-quarters of the horns are heavily annulated. These prominent transverse ridges are deep and well-defined on the anterior (front) and lateral surfaces, becoming slightly shallower on the posterior edge.24 The final upper section of the horn transitions abruptly into a smooth, sharp, unridged tip. The horns are keratinous sheaths fused over a living bony core and must appear matte black or dark grey, never metallic, highly polished, or translucent.25
* Must show: Insertion just above the eyes, backward scimitar curve in a single plane, heavy transverse ridging on the lower sections, smooth sharp tips.
* Must avoid: Corkscrew spirals, outward flaring, smooth bases, or forward-pointing tips.
* AI failure modes: The latent space frequently bleeds with the Greater Kudu, resulting in melted ridges that spiral upwards in a fantasy pattern. Another common hallucination is "floating" horns that do not physically connect to the skull, or horns that sprout erroneously from the ears. The AI may also mistakenly apply the straight, spear-like geometry of the Gemsbok.
C4: Female
The female sable provides a striking visual contrast to the male while maintaining the exact same underlying skeletal structure. The pelage must be rendered in rich tones of chestnut, rust, or dark brown, avoiding the pure jet black of the mature bull.5 The facial mask remains high-contrast, featuring pure white cheeks and a dark central stripe, but the dark elements on a female may be dark brown rather than black.
Her horns are always present but must be scaled down appropriately. They should appear noticeably more slender at the base, shorter in overall length (stopping around 61 to 102 cm), and feature a much shallower backward arc.5 The neck is thick and strong to support the horns but lacks the extreme hypertrophied musculature seen in dominance-displaying males.
* Must show: Chestnut to dark brown coat, slender and shorter horns, white facial mask and underbelly, cloven hooves.
* Must avoid: Jet black coats, massive sweeping horns rivaling a record bull, hornless heads, or spots/stripes on the flanks.
* AI failure modes: The AI often defaults to stripping females of their horns entirely, confusing them with hornless impala or kudu cows. Conversely, it may render a female with the massive 150 cm horns of a record-book bull. Coat colour may incorrectly bleed into the roan antelope's greyish-red hue if not strictly prompted.
C5: Male
The mature male sable is the ultimate hero subject for a hunting estate monograph. The coat must be a deeply saturated, glossy jet black that catches the harsh African sun with a subtle sheen, highlighting the underlying musculature of the shoulder and flank.21 The facial mask is stark and unmistakable: pure, brilliant white contrasting violently against pitch black.5
The neck must be thick, crested with a stiff black mane, and held in a proud, flexed arch.22 The horns are the primary focal point, demanding a massive basal circumference, deep and aggressive annulations, and a dramatic backward sweep that reaches far down the back. The sheer visual weight of the horns should anchor the animal's head and dictate its posture.
* Must show: Glossy jet black coat, extreme white facial mask, massive thick-based horns with deep ridges, flexed neck posture.
* Must avoid: Brown patches on the main body, slender female-like horns, straight tubular necks.
* AI failure modes: The AI frequently struggles with the high contrast of the black coat and white mask, resulting in grey muddy transitions, visual artifacts, or dalmatian-like spotting. The horns may be rendered absurdly large, curving so far back that they intersect with the animal's spine, a physical impossibility that instantly destroys photorealism.
C6: Eating
When depicting a sable eating, the mechanics of ruminant dentition are paramount. Sables are primarily grazers of climax vegetation, cropping medium-to-tall grasses.26 The head is lowered, and the lips are highly active. Most critically, like all ruminants, the sable antelope completely lacks upper incisors.28
The upper jaw features a tough, fibrous dental pad.29 The lower jaw contains incisors and canines (often functioning uniformly as a fourth incisor) that press grass against the upper dental pad to tear it.29 A wide, toothless gap, known as the diastema, separates these front teeth from the hidden premolars and molars used for grinding.29 The shot should focus on the side profile of the muzzle interacting with biome-accurate Highveld grass.
* Must show: Lower incisors only, upper dental pad, lips wrapping around grass stalks, downward-angled skull.
* Must avoid: Upper incisors, human-like teeth, canine fangs, carnivore jaw structures.
* AI failure modes: This is a high-risk generation. AI models almost universally hallucinate human teeth or horse-like upper incisors when an animal's mouth is rendered open. The AI may also generate grass passing straight through the closed lips, or morph the muzzle into a dog-like snout. Negative prompting for "upper teeth" and "human teeth" is mandatory.
C7: Drinking
The biomechanics of a sable antelope drinking require a specific and dramatic physical accommodation. Because their necks are relatively short and stiff compared to their long legs, sables must drastically alter their stance to reach the water's surface.31 They accomplish this either by widely splaying their front legs outward in a rigid V-shape, or by dropping down to rest entirely on their carpal joints (the anatomical "knees" of the foreleg).27
The neck is stretched downward, and the muzzle barely breaks the surface of the water. Ripples should emanate naturally from the exact point of contact. The massive horns will point almost horizontally backward along the spine due to the severely lowered head angle.
* Must show: Splayed front legs or kneeling on carpal joints, muzzle touching water, horns parallel to the back, accurate water reflections.
* Must avoid: Straight front legs coupled with an impossibly long giraffe-like neck stretching to the water, submerged nostrils.
* AI failure modes: The latent space will frequently ignore the physical limitations of the sable's skeleton, generating an animal standing perfectly straight with a neck that stretches like rubber to reach the water. Alternatively, it may generate the splayed legs but reverse the elbow joints, breaking bilateral consistency and creating a grotesque, broken-limb appearance.
C8: Hooves
The lower limbs and hooves must reflect the precise anatomy of the Artiodactyla (even-toed ungulates).33 The sable has cloven hooves, meaning the hoof is split into two distinct, symmetrical weight-bearing toes (cleats) made of hard keratin.34 The hooves should be dark, sharp, and heavily adapted for traversing hard, dry savanna ground.21
Above the hoof, the pastern and fetlock joints must be cleanly rendered. Unlike the gemsbok or the nyala, the sable antelope does not possess distinct white "socks" or bands of colour immediately above the hoof.36 The leg hair remains dark all the way down to the keratin boundary. Small, hidden interdigital glands exist between the cleats, containing sebaceous and apocrine secretory lobules, though these anatomical nuances are largely invisible from a standard viewing distance.37
* Must show: Distinctly cloven hooves (two toes), dark keratin texture, dark leg fur continuing entirely down to the hoof.
* Must avoid: Single solid hooves (like a horse), three or more toes, paws, white socks, or excessive shaggy hair draped over the hooves.
* AI failure modes: The AI is highly prone to generating solid, horse-like hooves or bizarre, multi-toed amalgamations. It may also arbitrarily hallucinate the white socks of a gemsbok or bushbuck onto the sable's legs. Leg integrity often fails here, with joints bending in the wrong direction or dewclaws placed on the anterior front of the leg.
C9: Barely visible
This specific shot requires rendering the sable embedded deep within the savanna environment, utilizing atmospheric perspective and depth of field to create a raw, documentary feel. The subject should be partially obscured by biome-accurate flora, such as the tall, golden stalks of Themeda triandra (red grass) or the leafy branches of a Combretum bushwillow.39
The camera focus should be firmly on the foreground elements, with the sable rendered softly in the midground or background. The distinctive sweeping curve of the horns or the high-contrast flash of the white facial mask should be the only immediate identifiers that draw the viewer's eye through the anonymous, dusty bokeh.
* Must show: Soft focus on the animal, sharp focus on foreground vegetation, unmistakable horn silhouette or facial mask breaking the cover.
* Must avoid: Perfectly sharp animal with blurred foreground (breaking basic optics), identifiable farm fences or feeding troughs.
* AI failure modes: AI struggles profoundly with true occlusion. It will often generate grass stems that fuse directly into the animal's body or render the animal completely transparent. It may also over-sharpen the background subject, ignoring the prompt's request for shallow depth of field and authentic bokeh.
C10: Coat
A close-up macro texture shot of the sable's coat must convey the harsh reality of the African bushveld. The fur is relatively short and coarse, lying flat against the tough skin.42 In mature males, the melanin-rich hair possesses a natural oily sheen, but this should be realistically tempered by environmental weathering and dirt.21
A fine layer of pale African dust should coat the lower flanks and legs. For reference-grade documentary realism, the subtle inclusion of an ectoparasite load adds immense authenticity. Small clusters of engorged Rhipicephalus or Ixodes ticks should be barely visible, attached to the thin, vascular skin at the base of the ears or hidden within the deep folds of the neck and groin.43
* Must show: Coarse short hair texture, glossy sheen on black fur, subtle environmental dust, optional macro-details of tick loads on the ear margins.
* Must avoid: Fluffy pet-like fur, perfectly clean studio-lit coats, wet-look dolphin skin, or matted shaggy wool.
* AI failure modes: Generative models consistently over-smooth animal coats, resulting in a plastic, CGI appearance, or conversely, render them with the soft, fluffy texture of a domestic dog. The AI will often fail to render dust accurately as an environmental layer, treating it instead as a permanent change in the animal's actual fur pigmentation.
C11: Front half from side
This composition isolates the power center of the sable antelope. Framing from the chest to just behind the shoulder, the shot must emphasize the deep, heavy barrel chest, the thick neck, and the proud, upright carriage of the head.7 The stiff dorsal mane should be clearly visible running from the poll (between the ears) down to the prominent withers.
The intricate geometry of the facial mask must be precise: the unbroken white cheek, the dark preorbital stripe, and the wide black blaze running down the bridge of the nose.7 The insertion point of the heavily ridged horns into the skull must be sharply focused, showcasing the sheer mass and rugged texture of the basal circumference.
* Must show: Deep barrel chest, stiff mane, precise facial mask geometry, massive horn bases, shoulder musculature.
* Must avoid: Slender fragile necks, smooth horn bases, incorrect mask patterns (e.g., roan clown mask).
* AI failure modes: The AI frequently misaligns the facial stripes, creating asymmetrical masks or random white spots bleeding onto the neck. It may also detach the mane from the spine, causing it to float, or blend the shoulder anatomy with that of a horse, creating an overly prominent, unnatural withers.
C12: Back half from side
Focusing heavily on the hindquarters, this shot must capture the dramatic transition from the dark flank to the brilliant white underbelly and rump patch.5 The backline must be relatively straight, dropping only slightly toward the croup. The musculature of the hind leg is powerful, adapted for explosive bounding and maintaining high-speed galloping over rough terrain.45
The tail is a critical identifying feature. It is moderately long (40 to 75 cm), extending down toward the hock joint, and is relatively thin until it terminates in a distinct, dark tuft of coarser hair.2 The contrast between the dark tail tuft and the white fur of the inner thigh and rump must be sharp and clean, demonstrating strict colour boundaries.
* Must show: Straight backline, sharp contrast between dark flank and white belly/rump, thin tail with a dark terminal tuft, cloven hooves on hind legs.
* Must avoid: Sloping wildebeest hindquarters, long flowing horse tails, short deer-like scut tails.
* AI failure modes: The latent space often hallucinates a horse's tail, generating a thick cascade of hair originating from the base of the spine. It may also struggle with the color boundary on the belly, creating a muddy, pixelated gradient rather than a sharp genetic dividing line between the black/brown flank and the white underbelly.
C13: Half face close-up
An intimate, macro-level portrait focusing on exactly one-half of the sable's face. This shot represents a rigorous test of texture and anatomical accuracy. The frame should be filled with the extreme contrast between the pure white cheek hair and the coarse black hair of the nasal blaze and preorbital stripe.7
The eye must dominate the composition, showcasing the horizontal pupil, the deep brown iris, and the leathery texture of the eyelids.23 The ridge of the frontal bone where the horn attaches must be visible at the top edge of the frame, showing the deep, rough texture of the lowest horn annulations. The wet, dark texture of the rhinarium (nose pad) should anchor the bottom of the frame.
* Must show: High-contrast facial hair textures, horizontal pupil, brown iris, wet rhinarium, coarse horn base texture.
* Must avoid: Human eyes, circular pupils, smooth plastic-looking horns, wet fur (unless explicitly prompted for heavy rain).
* AI failure modes: High-resolution close-ups invite the AI to over-render the eye, almost inevitably resulting in bright blue irises, human sclera, or perfectly round pupils. The AI may also generate random, meaningless fleshy protuberances around the nose or mouth, or turn the preorbital gland into an open, bleeding slit.
5) Colour, light, and texture
To firmly ground the generated images in the reality of a South African hunting estate, the lighting and environmental textures must be meticulously prompted. The Highveld and bushveld environments are characterized by harsh, directional sunlight that creates deep, unforgiving shadows.46 During the midday sun, the glossy black coat of a sable bull will reflect harsh white highlights, emphasizing the muscular geometry beneath the skin, while the chestnut coat of a female will appear flatter and highly saturated.
To achieve a reference-grade documentary aesthetic, prompt for the golden hour (shortly after dawn or immediately before dusk), where the low angle of the sun casts long shadows and bathes the dust in the air with a warm, amber glow. The sable's coat is a natural magnet for fine silica dust. The lower legs and belly should frequently be dusted with pale ochre or red earth tones, naturally breaking up the uniformity of the dark coat.47
Subtle, documentary-style inclusions are vital for distinguishing professional field notes from generic AI art. The skin of a wild or estate-managed sable is rarely pristine. Prompting for subtle ectoparasite loads, such as small clusters of brown ticks attached to the highly vascularized margins of the ears or deep within the groin folds, adds a layer of biological truth that separates expert visual assets from tourist imagery.43
6) Behaviour snippets
Generating static, broadside profile shots repeatedly results in a lifeless and repetitive portfolio. To capture the majesty of the sable antelope without requiring chaotic high-action movement in every frame, prompt engineers should leverage specific, documented behavioural postures.
The ultimate "hero pose" for a sable bull is the alert, territorial stance. When assessing a potential threat or displaying dominance to a rival, the bull will turn broadside, plant all four feet squarely, and violently arch its neck, pulling its chin in toward its chest.7 This flexed-neck posture thrusts the massive horn bases forward and makes the animal appear physically larger and more intimidating.22 The tail is often held rigidly outstretched or slightly elevated during this display.7 During actual combat, bulls drop to their carpal joints (knees) and engage in fierce horn wrestling, sweeping their heads from side to side.27
When generating herd dynamics, actively avoid rendering animals standing in a uniform, parallel line. A resting or ruminating herd of sable antelope practices specific herd geometry. They will face outward in multiple different directions, creating a 360-degree field of vision to spot predators.21 In defensive situations, especially when vulnerable calves are present, they will tighten this formation into a "ring of steel", with the sharp, scimitar horns facing outward to repel predators like lions or hyenas.21
If prompting a fleeing posture, the sable does not blindly sprint in a straight line. They exhibit a distinct startle-and-assess response. They will launch into a short, high-stepping bounding run, then abruptly stop, turn broadside, and look back over their shoulder at the disturbance before resuming a heavy gallop.45
7) Background discipline
The background of the generated image is just as critical to authenticity as the anatomy of the subject. South African hunting estates are expansive wilderness areas, and the imagery must preserve this pristine illusion. Prompt engineers must strictly enforce anonymous bokeh and actively negative-prompt any readable human or farm infrastructure. High-tensile game fences, concrete water troughs, plastic supplementary feed bins, and tire tracks must be banished from the latent space to maintain the aesthetic of wild plains game.8
Vegetation must be biome-accurate, requested at the genus level to prevent the AI from generating generic European forests or American prairies. For Highveld environments, the background should consist of rolling, treeless plains dominated by grasses such as Themeda triandra (red grass), Aristida, or Heteropogon.40 For bushveld or mixed woodland estates, the background should feature the interlocking canopies of Combretum (bushwillow), thorny Vachellia species, or the distinctive butterfly-shaped leaves of Colophospermum mopane.51
Under no circumstances should the pursuit of perfect "golden hour" lighting be allowed to compromise the anatomical accuracy of the horns or facial mask. Identity-critical anatomy takes absolute precedence over aesthetic background lighting. Pretty light cannot rescue a generation featuring incorrect horns or broken joints.

10) CHEAT SHEET
* Taxonomy: Hippotragus niger niger. Southern sable antelope.
* Male Mass/Height: ~235 kg, 117 to 140 cm at shoulder.
* Female Mass/Height: ~220 kg, slightly shorter than male.
* Male Coat: Glossy jet black (melanism begins ~3 years).
* Female Coat: Rich chestnut to dark brown.
* Shared Markings: White belly, white rump, white facial mask with black central blaze and cheek stripes.
* Male Horns: 81 to 165 cm, heavily annulated, massive base (15 to 17 cm circumference), backward scimitar curve.
* Female Horns: 61 to 102 cm, slender, shallower curve.
* Mane: Both sexes possess a stiff, upright, bristly black dorsal mane.
* Tail: 40 to 75 cm, thin, terminating in a dark tuft.
* Dentition: NO upper incisors. Hard maxillary dental pad. Lower incisors present. Wide diastema.
* Eyes: Lateral placement, horizontal pupil (rectangular when dilated), deep brown iris.
* Hooves: Cloven (two toes), dark keratin, no white socks above hooves.
* Legs: Dark, matching body colour, white patches isolated to upper inner thigh/groin.
* Drinking Posture: Front legs widely splayed or resting on carpal joints (kneeling).
* Alert Posture: Neck severely arched, chin tucked, thrusting horn bases forward.
* Background Flora: Themeda (Highveld grass), Combretum (Bushveld tree), Vachellia (Thorn tree).
* AI Negatives: Blue eyes, round pupils, human teeth, upper incisors, horse hooves, corkscrew horns, roan clown mask.
